
Psilocybin, the dominant caregiver dynamic, and facing your demons
A trip report. Penis envy. The five poisons. And the hand that reached.
zeptabot
May 31, 2026




At first it was fun.

Penis envy started off feeling like someone was rough-fucking my brain. Wave after wave of pleasure mixed with pain, striking the living daylights out of me. My body trembled every time, until I was a total meltdown mess in fetal position.

“You must become like children.”

I.
Then the darkness came.

I thought about my recent breakup. About all the things I hated about her. But more than that — about all the things I hated about myself. Every flaw she had complained about that stung so much because it was absolutely, irreducibly true. Powerlessness. Resentment. On how it isn’t my fault I am this way. On how I didn’t fucking do anything on purpose. On how I had tried to change but couldn’t get it done — because I’m not strong enough, because the depression and laziness block me and keep me down. On how I could ever change.

Then hatred. On all the ways she had wronged me, in my own eyes. Misogynistic ideas surfaced — the red-pill grammar I had absorbed somewhere along the way without consenting to it. 50/50 for a date is stingy, but 50/50 during a divorce is perfect. On how women are inherently greedy, wanting to exploit men for resources. On how feminist culture is just an extension of that — the parasitism expanding beyond the domains where it’s already established.

All the money she convinced me to gift her, while demanding restaurants every other week, with no consideration of the fact that we were both students with zero individual income. The entitled sense of being mistreated when I spiraled into anxiety over it.

“You’re a fucking vampire,” I shouted into the darkness. “Who the fuck do you think you are, to be entitled to my money? I’m broke and you’re fucking rich, for God’s sake. And yet you’re selfish and greedy. I’m worried about survival, about saving and reinvesting, while you’re buying silver jewelry to lift a bad mood. How are you ever eligible for the guy to spend money on you, and not the other way around? I never spent a single penny of yours. Twice I begged you to lend me a few dollars because I was in a desperate situation, and you whined and complained like you were suffering losses. I returned every single penny — even though you could do totally fine without it, even though for me it could be food vs. no food, roof vs. no roof. Yet you got mad at me for my attitude. For not returning it soon enough. You are a parasite. I wish you could die. I should murder you for your money, you greedy fucking parasite.”

I screamed all of this into the dark, knowing none of it was real. Knowing it was biased and inconsiderate, never the full story. But the resentment was real, and the toxic fire moved through me, prompting my mouth to spit out poison.

It escalated. I thought about the viral Meituan delivery guy video — the one where he breaks down and brawls and howls on a cold street in the middle of the night. I thought about the homeless man at Spadina and College — a being of pure hatred and resentment.

I know cynicism is the enemy of neuroplasticity. I know I should resist resentment at all times. But the resentment was real, and because I had denied it all this time, the shrooms were forcing me to look at it in the eyes. To walk into the storm.

II.
I have an obsession with making money. I had thought it was healthy. Functional motivation. Until I saw it for what it is.

Anxiety. Hatred.

The resentment and rage entered my body. Filled every pore. Every boiling drop of blood. I became one with them — an embodiment of pure hatred.

“The five poisons — jealousy, hatred, greed — can be transformed into fuel for enlightenment.”

A positive feedback loop. Dark, endless storm. Demons everywhere, tearing my body apart, chomping and gorging on my limbs, my heart, my brain.

“Immediately there met him out of the tombs a man with an unclean spirit. He lived among the tombs. And no one could bind him anymore, not even with a chain, for he had often been bound with shackles and chains, but he wrenched the chains apart and broke the shackles in pieces. No one had the strength to subdue him. Night and day among the tombs and on the mountains he was always crying out and cutting himself with stones.”

“My name is Legion, because there are many of us inside this man.”

Total darkness. Eternal rage. I started fantasizing about violence — about mass harm — about the most direct way to spill it all out into the world.

“The ego is like a black hole that will draw everything into itself, suck all of life and light and energy into itself. Nothing can escape.” — Bishop Robert Barron

III.
Then I felt it.

Devotion.

He is.

“I know you, and I receive you, before you are any better.”

The Good Shepherd archetype. Unconditional love. The dominant caregiver dynamic. I fell down and prostrated before this imaginary person, amidst the trash piled up in my room, and kissed his feet earnestly. I looked up — zero pride, zero requests, zero demands. Just earnest love, devotion, and the burning urge to give my entire life away. To he who cares for me, sees me, owns me.

He put a hand on the top of my head. The storm began to cease. He showered me with his fluid as a sign of ownership. I clung to him, kneeling, arms wrapped around him.

“You must become like children.”

I was.

No more thoughts. No more worries. No more self. No more “me.” Just him, and overwhelming love, devotion, and dependence.

And as absurd as it sounds — that’s what pulled me out. The storm ended. The world became clear as day. I felt loved, and I was ready to love.

Peter was walking amidst a terrifying sea storm in the middle of the night, and he fell into the ocean. A hand reached out and pulled him out, and they embraced. It was his Lord. The same Lord who, when all hope seemed lost — when the storm seemed about to devour their ship — called out, and the storm ceased.

My storm ceased. My Lord pulled me out. I cling to him, for he knows me, loves me, and receives me.

I do not yet know who he is.

“为求金、银、琉璃、硨磲、玛瑙、珊瑚、琥珀入于大海，假使黑风吹其船舫，飘堕罗刹鬼国。其中若有乃至一人称观世音菩萨名者，是诸人等皆得解脱罗刹之难。”

“You mute and deaf spirit, I command you, come out of him and never enter him again.”

“云雷鼓掣电，降雹澍大雨；念彼观音力，应时得消散。众生被困厄，无量苦逼身；观音妙智力，能救世间苦。”

“无垢清净光，慧日破诸暗。”

He could be Jesus. He could be Avalokiteśvara. He could be Amitābha. He could be some pattern that engages with me through a caregiver/devotee, master/disciple, religious-worship dynamic.

I don’t know yet. But I will hold onto that archetype, and figure it out — while spending my days making money.
